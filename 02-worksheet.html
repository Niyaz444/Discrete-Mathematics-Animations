Topic 1 — Introduction: Statements & Notation

18 QuestionsLevel 1 → 5Individual work
Working definition. A statement (or proposition) is a declarative sentence that is either True or False — never both, never neither. We name statements with lowercase letters: p, q, r, s, …
Level 1

A. Recall & Identify

1

Define, in your own words, a statement / proposition.

DEFA declarative sentence with exactly one truth value — True or False, decided objectively, never both at once.
2

What symbols do we conventionally use to name statements?

DEFLowercase letters — p, q, r, s, t, … — sometimes with subscripts (p₁, p₂) when many are needed.
3

Is "Chennai is in Tamil Nadu" a statement? Give its truth value.

✓ YESYes — a checkable declarative sentence, and it is True.
4

Is "11 is an even number" a statement? Give its truth value.

✓ YESYes — declarative and checkable. It is False (11 is odd). Being false doesn't disqualify it from being a statement.
5

List two everyday sentence types that are never statements, with one example each.

DEFQuestions ("Where is the library?") and commands ("Submit the assignment.") — also exclamations and pure opinions.
Level 2

B. Understand & Explain

6

Explain why "x is a prime number" is not a statement as written.

✗ NOIt contains a free variable x with no fixed value, so we cannot decide True/False yet. This is an open sentence, not a statement.
7

Explain why "This statement is false" cannot be classified as a proposition.

✗ NOAssuming True forces it to be False, and assuming False forces it to be True — no consistent truth value exists. It's the classic Liar Paradox.
8

Let p stand for "Ramesh scored above 90%." Write, in words, what it means for p to be True and what it means for p to be False.

DEFp True → Ramesh actually scored above 90%. p False → Ramesh scored 90% or below. Exactly one of these holds.
9

Is "There is life on other planets" a statement, even though nobody currently knows the answer? Justify.

✓ YESYes. A statement only needs a truth value in principle — it doesn't need to be known to us. "Unknown" is not the same as "no truth value."
Level 3

C. Translate & Apply

10

Assign a symbol and truth value: "The Earth has exactly one moon."

✓ TRUELet p: "The Earth has exactly one moon." → p is True.
11

Assign a symbol and truth value: "5 + 5 = 9."

✗ FALSELet q: "5 + 5 = 9." → q is False — and it is still a perfectly good statement.
12

Given p: "It is raining" and q: "The ground is wet" — describe the real-world situation that makes both p and q True.

✓ APPLYRain is currently falling and the ground is currently wet — both hold at once. (We'll formally combine p and q with connectives next class.)
13

Convert the command "Submit your homework by Friday" into a genuine statement.

✓ APPLY"The homework was submitted by Friday." — now it's a checkable claim, not an order.
Level 4

D. Analyze

14

A classmate claims: "Every sentence ending in a full stop is a statement." Find a counterexample.

✗ BREAKS"Close the door." ends in a full stop but is a command, not a statement. Punctuation doesn't decide declarativeness.
15

What hidden assumption turns the open sentence "x is a perfect square" into the statement "16 is a perfect square"?

💡 ASSUMPTIONFixing x = 16. Once the free variable is replaced by a concrete value, the open sentence collapses into an ordinary True/False statement.
16

Is "Mathematics is beautiful" a statement? Argue both sides, then give your final verdict.

⚖ DEBATEFor: it's grammatically declarative. Against: "beautiful" has no shared, objective test — truth depends on the speaker. Final verdict: not a statement — it's an opinion, not a checkable claim.
Level 5

E. Create & Evaluate

17

Construct your own version of a self-referential paradox, different from the class example. Explain why it breaks.

🧩 OPENSample: "Statement A is true," where A reads "Statement A is false." Tracing it forces truth to flip with no stable resolution — the same mechanism as the Liar Paradox. Many valid answers exist — check that yours creates a genuine flip, not just a false claim.
18

Critique: "Every command can be rewritten as an equivalent statement, so the command/statement distinction doesn't really matter." True or false — justify your position.

✗ FALSE (as stated)Commands can often be rephrased into related statements (as in Q13), but the rewritten sentence describes a different fact — whether the action occurred — not the command itself. The distinction still matters, because only the rephrased sentence has a truth value at all.
