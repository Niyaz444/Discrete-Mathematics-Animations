Topic 1 — Introduction: Statements & Notation

Admit to Logic

10 quick-fire questions. Answer from instinct — we haven't officially covered this yet.

5 min
1Lv1

True or False (of the sentence itself): "Shut the laboratory door." — can this sentence be labelled True or False?

✗ NOIt's a command, not a claim about the world. There's nothing to check, so it can't be true or false.
2Lv1

Which of these could you mark "right" or "wrong" on a test: "The sum of the angles of a triangle is 180°" or "Geometry is the most elegant branch of mathematics"?

✓ FIRSTThe triangle sentence describes a checkable fact. The "most elegant" sentence is a matter of opinion — no universal test decides it.
3Lv1

In everyday speech, when someone "makes a statement," what are they usually doing? (a) asking something (b) asserting a claim (c) giving an order

(b)Asserting a claim — declaring that something is (or isn't) the case. That instinct is exactly what mathematicians formalise.
4Lv2

Guess: can "y − 3 = 4" be called True or False right now, before y is told to you?

✗ NOT YETIts truth depends entirely on the unknown y. Sentences like this are called open sentences — we'll meet them properly later.
5Lv2

Try to pin one truth value on: "This very sentence is false." Can you do it without contradicting yourself?

✗ IMPOSSIBLESay it's true → it must be false. Say it's false → it must be true. It flips forever. This is the famous Liar Paradox — a preview of why logicians are careful about what counts as a statement.
6Lv2

Quick sort — is this a statement or not: "New Delhi is the capital of India."

✓ YESA checkable, declarative claim — and True. That's the shape every statement will have.
7Lv3

Predict: mathematicians often replace a whole sentence with a single letter, like p. Why might that be useful, beyond just saving ink?

💡 IDEAShort symbols let us combine and manipulate claims with fixed rules — without getting distracted by what the sentence is actually about.
8Lv3

Why do you think opinions ("this is the best theorem") cause trouble if we try to build a logical system out of them?

💡 IDEALogic needs a truth value everyone can agree to check the same way. Opinions have no shared, objective test — so there's no fixed truth value to reason with.
9Lv4

Can a question ever be rewritten so it becomes a genuine statement? If so, how?

✓ YES"Is it raining?" isn't a statement — but rewritten as "It is raining," it becomes one, checkable and assignable a truth value. The question form itself is what disqualifies it.
10Lv5

One-line guess: based on the word predicate, what do you think "predicate calculus" studies?

🔮 PREVIEW"Predicate" = what's said about a subject (e.g. "___ is even"). By the end of this module, we'll formalise statements with unknowns like x — that's predicate calculus. Hold onto your guess!
