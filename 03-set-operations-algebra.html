3.1

Five Operations

Every way of combining two sets reduces to one of these five moves.

Union
A ∪ B = {x | x∈A or x∈B}

Everything in A, in B, or in both.

Intersection
A ∩ B = {x | x∈A and x∈B}

Only what's shared by both.

Complement
A′ = {x ∈ U | x∉A}

Everything in the universe U that A leaves out.

Difference
A − B = {x | x∈A and x∉B}

What's in A, once everything shared with B is removed.

Symmetric Difference
A △ B = (A−B) ∪ (B−A)

Everything in exactly one of the two sets — never both.

Three Real-Life Examples

Union, intersection, and difference in the wild.

Union — Sports Club Sign-Up

The set of students who play cricket OR football (or both) is exactly who shows up for the joint sports briefing — that's C ∪ F.

C ∪ F

Intersection — Cross-Sell Report

An online store's marketing team wants customers who bought both a phone AND a case — the overlap is exactly who gets the "complete your set" email.

Phone ∩ Case

Difference — HR Shortlisting

An HR filter for "has a degree but no prior work experience" is precisely Degree − Experienced — everyone in the first group, minus anyone who's also in the second.

Degree − Experienced

3.5

Two Counter-Examples

Two claims that look plausible — and both are wrong.

False Claim

"Set difference is commutative: A − B = B − A."

False. With A = {1,2,3,4,5} and B = {4,5,6,7,8}: A − B = {1,2,3} but B − A = {6,7,8} — completely different sets. Difference cares about direction: "what's in A but not B" is not the same question as "what's in B but not A."
False Claim

"The complement of a union is the union of the complements: (A ∪ B)′ = A′ ∪ B′."

False — this is the single most common De Morgan's mistake. With U = {1,…,9}, A = {1,2,3,4,5}, B = {4,5,6,7,8}: (A∪B)′ = {9}, but A′∪B′ = {1,2,3,6,7,8,9} — nowhere close. The correct law flips the connective: (A∪B)′ = A′ ∩ B′ (verified in the Algebra section below).
