In your own words: what two conditions must a sentence satisfy to be a statement?

DEFIt must be declarative (an assertion, not a question/command), and it must have exactly one truth value, True or False.
2Lv2

Classify: "Prove that √2 is irrational."

✗ NOT A STATEMENTIt's a command/instruction ("Prove that…") — not a declarative claim with a truth value.
3Lv3

Let p: "Two is a prime number." State the truth value of p.

✓ TRUE2 is prime (only divisible by 1 and itself), so p is True.
4Lv3

Is "y − 4 = 0" a statement? If not, what is it called?

✗ NONot a statement — y is a free variable. It's an open sentence.
5Lv4

Go back to your Entry Ticket Q5 (the self-referential sentence). Now, formally, why does it fail to be a statement?

✗ REJECTEDSelf-reference forces its truth value to flip endlessly — no stable assignment of True or False satisfies it, which violates the "exactly one truth value" requirement.
6Lv4

A friend argues: "If nobody knows whether it's true, it can't be a statement." What's the flaw in this reasoning?

✗ FLAWEDIt confuses our knowledge of the truth value with the truth value existing. A statement only needs a determinate truth value in principle — "unknown to us" is not the same as "undefined."
