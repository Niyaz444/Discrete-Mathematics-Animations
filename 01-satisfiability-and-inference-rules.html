Part 1

Satisfiable or Unsatisfiable?

A formula is Satisfiable if some assignment makes it True. It's Unsatisfiable if no assignment ever can.

Satisfiable: there exists at least one assignment of truth values that makes the formula TRUE.

Unsatisfiable: there is no assignment that makes the formula true — it's always FALSE.

Part 2

Theory of Inference

An inference rule draws a conclusion from evidence. Read the rule, try to invent your own example, then reveal the trick and a ready-made one.

Premises (P₁, P₂, …) are statements you're given — simple or compound. The Conclusion (∴ C) is what you're allowed to derive from them.

Checking validity: if P₁∧P₂∧…∧Pₙ → C is a Tautology, the rule is a genuine inference rule (T = IR ✓). If it can ever be False, it's a Fallacy (F ≠ IR ✗).
