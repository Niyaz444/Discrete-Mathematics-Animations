Wrapping Up Connectives

Before you go — show me what stuck. Compare your Entry Ticket instincts to what you now know formally.

5 min
1Lv2

List all five connectives covered today with their symbols.

DEFNegation ¬, Conjunction ∧, Disjunction ∨, Conditional →, Biconditional ↔.
2Lv2

Given p = True, q = True, evaluate p ↔ q.

TRUEBoth share the same truth value (both True), so the biconditional holds.
3Lv3

Let p: "the alarm rings," q: "I wake up." Translate: "The alarm rings but I don't wake up."

✓p ∧ ¬q — "but" behaves just like "and" in logic.
4Lv3

Given p = False, q = True, evaluate p → q.

TRUEThe conditional is only False when p is True and q is False. Here p is already False, so it's automatically True.
5Lv4

Return to your Entry Ticket Q9 (rain / umbrella). Now, formally: what truth value does p → q take when p is False, no matter what q is?

ALWAYS TRUEWhenever the antecedent is False, the conditional is vacuously True — this matches your entry-ticket instinct that the sentence "makes no promise" when it doesn't rain.
6Lv4

A classmate says p ∨ q and p ⊕ q are interchangeable. Give the one truth-value combination where they disagree.

DISAGREEWhen p = True and q = True: p ∨ q = True, but p ⊕ q = False.
7Lv5

Write a compound sentence from your own life using a biconditional (↔), and explain what it takes for your sentence to be True.

🧩 OPENSample: "I get a certificate if and only if I attend all sessions." True exactly when both halves match — either both happen, or neither does. Many valid answers.
8Lv3

Translate: "Neither the printer nor the scanner is working," using p: "printer works," q: "scanner works."

✓¬p ∧ ¬q — "neither... nor" negates both and joins them with "and."
9Lv5

Predict: next class we ask whether a compound statement can be True no matter what truth values its parts take. What do you think such a statement would be called?

🔮 NEXTA tautology — a compound statement that comes out True in every single row of its truth table. That's exactly Topic 3.
