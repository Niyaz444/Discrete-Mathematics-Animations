Proving Without Truth Tables

Problem 1.16 — every step is a known law of equivalence. Read each line, then identify the law that justifies the next one.

How this works: each proof is a chain of equivalent expressions. Between two lines sits a hidden question — which law turned the line above into the line below? Pick an option, then check yourself. This is exactly the skill behind proving implications algebraically instead of building an 8-row truth table.

Reference — Laws of Equivalence

A∨F ≡ A  ·  A∧T ≡ A — Identity Law
A∨T ≡ T  ·  A∧F ≡ F — Domination Law
A∨A ≡ A  ·  A∧A ≡ A — Idempotent Law
A∨¬A ≡ T  ·  A∧¬A ≡ F — Negation Law
¬¬A ≡ A — Double Negation Law
A∨B ≡ B∨A — Commutative Law
A∧(B∨C) ≡ (A∧B)∨(A∧C) — Distributive Law
¬(A∨B) ≡ ¬A∧¬B — De Morgan's Law
A→B ≡ ¬A∨B — Implication Law
A∨(A∧B) ≡ A — Absorption Law
A→A ≡ T — Self-Implication
